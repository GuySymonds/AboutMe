# Engineering Practices

## Summary

Deep experience defining, improving, and embedding engineering practices across teams and organisations. Focused on raising quality, reducing delivery risk, and making engineering more predictable and maintainable.

## Key Points

- Agile / Scrum / Kanban coaching and facilitation
- Defining engineering standards, quality policies, and technical decision frameworks
- Cross-team collaboration, dependency management, and coordination patterns
- Code review processes, PR standards, and quality gates
- Test strategy: unit, integration, contract, and end-to-end testing
- Branching strategies and release management
- Technical onboarding and reducing cost of change for new engineers
- Retrospectives, continuous improvement, and team health checks

## Detail

Comfortable setting practices at an organisation level — defining what "good" looks like and helping teams adopt it — as well as working hands-on with teams to improve specific workflows.

### Specific Practices

- **Branching and release:** trunk-based development, feature flags, conventional commit standards, semantic versioning
- **Quality improvement:** test coverage uplift, static analysis, linting, enforced review processes
- **Standards definition:** API design standards, logging and observability guidelines, security checklists
- **Cross-team flow:** API contracts, consumer-driven contract testing, dependency planning in refinement
- **Delivery rhythm:** sprint cadence, definition of done, release checklists, post-incident reviews

### Selected Outcomes

- Reduced production defects by 60% through improved test coverage and quality standards at NewDay.
- Rebuilt delivery predictability at Elekta through modern branching/release strategy and engineering standards, replacing risky big-bang releases with incremental service delivery.
- Simplified tooling and workflows at Elekta to reduce onboarding friction, test complexity, and cost of change.
- Improved cross-team delivery flow at NewDay by defining clearer API/contract boundaries and introducing better dependency planning.
